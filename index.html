No.01

On the Weight of a Matcha Bowl

Object — Ceramic — Ritual

A good chawan is heavier than you expect the first time you hold it, and that is the entire point. The weight slows your hands down before the tea does anything to your head. Most packaging today is designed to disappear — light, frictionless, gone in one motion. A bowl built for matcha does the opposite: it insists on a pause.

What I keep noticing, watching people discover matcha as a category rather than a drink, is that the ritual sells the product more honestly than the marketing does. You can't rush a whisked bowl. The beauty is in the resistance.

No.02

The Discipline of Empty Space

Practice — Ikebana — Restraint

In ikebana, the branch you remove matters more than the one you keep. Beginners fill the vessel; the practice is learning to leave it alone. What looks like emptiness is actually the hardest decision in the arrangement — the negative space is doing as much work as the flower.

I think about this constantly in brand work, where the instinct is always to add one more line, one more offer, one more banner. The strongest campaigns I've shipped were the ones I fought to keep empty.

No.03

Why a Kombucha Bottle Deserves a Second Look

Object — Glass — Category

Launching a fermented drink in a new city means fighting for shelf space against products people already trust. The bottle has to do the convincing before anyone reads the label. What I learned launching MOR'KO in Hamburg is that glass has a memory — the thickness, the neck taper, the way light sits in it — and shoppers read that memory instinctively, faster than copy.

Beauty, in a fridge full of competitors, is a shortcut to trust.

No.04

The Kerning of a Title Card

Film — Typography — First Impressions

Before a single frame of story, a film tells you how much it respects you through its opening title. Loose kerning, a lazy stock font, a rushed fade — you know within four seconds what kind of attention went into the rest. The directors I return to are, without exception, the ones who treated the title card like a promise.

Brands make the same promise in their first five seconds. Most break it immediately.

About

Barcelona

I'm Maryna Savchenko, a brand and growth marketing manager currently building the matcha category at NaturaleBio — overseeing ecommerce strategy, CRM, social, influencer partnerships, and everything in between that a "brand manager" title doesn't quite cover anymore.

What I think sets me apart in this field isn't a framework — it's taste. A trained eye for what actually looks and feels right, built the same way any craft is built: by paying close attention, over years, to objects that work.

Outside of work I practice ikebana, and I'm hoping to exhibit arrangements in a few Barcelona galleries soon. I also write about film, off and on, at putthelightsdown.wordpress.com. This site is where those two habits — looking closely, and writing it down — meet the working part of my life.
